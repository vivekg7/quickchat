# Privacy

QuickChat collects nothing and sends nothing.

- **No network access.** The app does not declare the `INTERNET` permission, so it
  cannot connect to anything. There are no ads, analytics, crash reporters or
  accounts.
- **What stays on the device.** Your settings, the country code you picked, and — if
  "Keep recent numbers" is on — the numbers you opened chats with, the app you used
  and any labels you added. These live in the app's private storage. They are
  excluded from Android backup and device-to-device transfer, and are deleted when you
  clear them in Settings or uninstall the app.
- **Clipboard.** When the app opens it may look at the clipboard for a phone number to
  suggest (Android shows a "pasted from your clipboard" notice when it does). It reads
  the clipboard only while it is on screen, skips content marked sensitive, and never
  stores what it reads. You can turn this off in Settings. The paste button and the
  Quick Settings tile read the clipboard only when you use them.
- **Hand-off to messengers.** When you tap "Open in …", QuickChat opens that app with
  its official click-to-chat link containing the number (and, for WhatsApp, the
  message you typed). What happens next is governed by that messenger's own privacy
  policy. For Telegram and Signal, a message you typed is copied to the clipboard so
  you can paste it.
- **Installed apps.** QuickChat checks whether WhatsApp, WhatsApp Business, Telegram
  (or Telegram X) and Signal (or Molly) are installed, to show the right buttons. It
  cannot see any other apps.
